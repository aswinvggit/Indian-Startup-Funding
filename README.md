# Indian-Startup-Funding

## Project Objective

The objective of this project is to analyze Indian startup funding data to understand funding trends, popular industries, major investors, and key startup hubs in India.

## Project Definition

This project analyzes a dataset containing funding details of Indian startups, including funding date, city, investors, industry, and amount invested. The analysis focuses on identifying patterns in startup funding over time, the most funded sectors and cities, and the role of major investors in the Indian startup ecosystem.

## Libraries

pandas is used for data loading and manipulation.
numpy supports numerical operations.
matplotlib, plotly and seaborn are used for data visualization and plotting.

## Dataset

The dataset has 3044 rows and 10 columns, contains funding information for Indian startups, includes details such as the funding date, the city where the startup is based, the names of the investors, and the amount invested (in USD).

Insights
1. Indian startups are mostly from the "consumer internet" industry.

We could say that due to the rapid growth of smartphone usage, affordable internet access and a large young population, indian startups are largely concentrated on the consumer internet industry. The widespread adoption of digital platforms has created a strong demand for services such as e-commerce, food delivery, online education...etc. As a result, many indian entrepreneurs focus on consumer-facing digital solutions that address everyday needs.

2. Dominance of "seed funding" and "private equity".

It shows strong investor interest at both early and growth stages. It reflects confidence in new startup ideas as well as in scaling successful startups.

3. High funding in the "transportation" idustry.

The transportation industry has the highest average funding because it requires large capital for infrastructue, technology and operations. Investors are willing to invest more due to its high growth potential and large market demand.

4. Lower funding for Kerala startups.

Startups in Kerala receive less funding, possibly due to fewer investors, smaller startup networks and limited access to large-scale opportunities compared to bigger startup hubs.

5. Majority of startups are small-scale(Tier-3).

Most startups are from the Tier-3 category, which is below 100 crore USD. This shows that the majority of startups are still small and in early growth stages, with room to scale up over time.

6. Declining startup funding.

Total funding amount per year is decreasing, which suggests that investors are becoming more cautious. Startups may be facing tougher competition, higher risks or stricter funding conditions over time.

7. Many of startups don't disclose investor names.

We can assume that it's for to protect privacy, maintain flexibility and stay focused on building the business. Also Ratan Tata tops in funding many of the startups.

Conclusion
Most indian startups are small, consumer-focused, and early stage, relying on seed funding and private equity. Funding is highest in sectors like transportation but is declining overall, with some regions like Kerala receiving less support. This shows a growing yet cautious startup ecosystem with room to scale.
